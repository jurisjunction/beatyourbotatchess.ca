Teresa's Pawn Luktor Labs

Why

A chess bot for people, from a workshop that would rather be small.

A person, not a product

Much of what is online now is built to gather you: your clicks, your words, your games, fed to systems you never agreed to feed. Many people are tired of it, and some have given up on bots and AI altogether. We understand why.

This bot plays you. It does not harvest you. It keeps what it needs to play, and it tells you what that is.

Asked, not taken

Beyond a record of its games with no names in it, and a working log kept a month, nothing about you is kept unless you ask. What it keeps at your request lives on Lichess, where you can read it. Its picture of you, it will tell you whenever you ask. It never reads your games with anyone else.

Small, and owned by those who use it

Teresa's Pawn comes from a family workshop, not a platform. The old idea that the tools of a trade should belong to the people who practise it suits software too: a teacher's tools should be the teacher's, and a student's record the student's.

The human is the master

It is built by one person whose trade is teaching, and whose study is theology and canon law, not programming. An AI writes the code, at his direction and under his eye. He decides what it is for, what it may do and what it must never do; the machine does the work.

The Teacher mode we are building is meant to work the same way: set up by a human teacher and answering to them, never in their place.

As it used to be

Nothing is trained on your games. What shapes the bot is people: the players who tell us where it went wrong, the arguments on Discord, the letters. Tell us what you see, and we will read it.
